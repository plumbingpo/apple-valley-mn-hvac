How Rochester Homeowners Can Extend HVAC Life

Most mid-season breakdowns trace back to skipped maintenance, and the habits that prevent them are simple. Change your filter every one to three months, keep plants and shrubs at least two feet from the outdoor condenser, and hose off the condenser coil every spring. Then book a professional tune-up each spring and fall, one for cooling and one for heating, so our technicians can catch small problems while they're still cheap to fix.

Take a look at the age of your system. Its age tells you a lot. With regular service, most air conditioners reach 12 to 15 years, gas furnaces 15 to 20, and heat pumps 10 to 15. If your system is beyond that range and also shows rising bills, uneven temperatures, or repeated repairs, it's a strong candidate for a planned replacement, which is almost always cheaper and easier on everyone than a rushed emergency install when the first heat wave hits.

Small problems rarely stay small when they're ignored. Consider a weak capacitor: replacing it is a short, affordable repair, but if it's ignored for a season, it can take the compressor with it, and just like that a minor fix has become a full system replacement. That's why we encourage homeowners to save our number in their phone and call at the first sign of trouble. Catching it early saves money and prevents a few miserable nights.
